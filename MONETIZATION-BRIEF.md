# Loop: free for families, paid at the seam

**Proposal, Aug 2026.** This inverts the model currently shown on the concept page
(Family $7.99/mo, Connected included with the school's license). Nothing in the prototype has been
changed — read this, then decide.

---

## The model in one line

**The parent app is free forever. The school pays for the connection.**

Everything a parent generates is free. Everything the classroom generates is paid, and the school
buys it — not the family.

---

## Why this beats charging parents

**Parents don't buy software.** Consumer subscriptions for "how is my child doing" are discretionary,
and discretionary spend is the first thing cut. Category churn runs 3–6 months. Worse, the filter is
backwards: the families who most need developmental framing are the least likely to put $7.99/mo on a
card. A paid parent tier optimises for the parents who need Loop least.

**Free parents are the distribution.** A parent using Loop who wants school signal has exactly one
action available to them: ask their teacher about ClassCade. That is a warm inbound lead at zero
acquisition cost, generated by a user who is already bought in. Charging $7.99 filters out most of the
people who would generate those leads. **You are not selling a subscription; you are buying leads with
free software, and free is cheaper than any ad.**

**Schools already buy, and already have the budget line.** Family engagement, SEL, and parent
communication are existing line items — several of them federally reportable. ClassCade already sells
into this room.

**The value is asymmetric, and it points at the school.** For a parent, Loop is genuinely useful. For a
school it is: fewer confused parent emails, a measurable family-engagement number, and a renewal
differentiator. The school captures more value, so the school should pay.

**It completes ClassCade's structure.** Showdown is free for teachers — that is the wedge that wins
adoption. Loop Connected becomes **the first thing ClassCade charges for**, and it monetises a
footprint that already exists rather than one that has to be built.

## The line: what's free, what the school unlocks

The rule is clean enough to put on a slide:

> **Free = everything the parent generates. Paid = everything the classroom generates.**

| Free forever · parent-generated | Unlocked by the school's license · classroom-generated |
|---|---|
| The Whole Learner picture, built from home observations | Showdown signals feeding all six dimensions |
| Tonight's conversation — generic bank + home-grounded questions | **School-grounded questions** — the ones a parent couldn't have thought of |
| Today's 5-minute moment | *What happened at school* — the raw, auditable signal feed |
| **Ask / "I'm noticing something"** and all crisis resources | Notes from the teacher |
| Age and grade expectations ("is this normal?") | **The return channel** — one-tap context back to the teacher |
| Unlimited children, no per-child pricing | Conference questions built from real classroom data |

**Ask and the crisis resources are free permanently and unconditionally.** A parent worried about their
child at 11pm must never hit a paywall. This is not a growth tactic; it is the condition of being
allowed to build the feature at all.

The prototype already degrades exactly this way — flip the tier toggle and four of Maya's six
dimensions fall back to "Getting to know". The architecture for this model is built; only the price
tags move.

---

## What the free app does with the gap

**Show the hole honestly. Never blur it.**

A blurred paywall over a child's development read is manipulative and it will be described that way.
The free app should instead be plainly, visibly incomplete in a way that is *true*:

- `Curiosity — Getting to know Maya. Not enough signals yet.`
- In the conversation card: *"This is where a question from Maya's classroom would go."*
- On the School tab: the empty state names the teacher and offers **"Ask your school about ClassCade"**
  with a shareable one-pager.

The gap sells the upgrade better than a blur does, because it is the actual product truth: Loop
genuinely cannot see school without school. A parent who understands that becomes an advocate. A parent
who feels teased becomes a one-star review.

## Pricing the school side

ClassCade's published early-implementation research covers **1,167 schools across 342 districts in
31 states**. That footprint is the asset — Loop does not need new distribution, it needs to monetise
existing distribution.

Recommended shape: **per campus, annual, sold as an add-on to the existing ClassCade relationship.**
Per-campus beats per-student because it removes the roster-count negotiation and it is a smaller
number to approve. Show the per-student math in the room, but don't price on it.

At **$1,200 / campus / year** (≈ $2.67 per student at a 450-student elementary campus, comfortably
inside category norms for family-engagement tools):

| Conversion of existing footprint | Campuses | ARR |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 117 | ~$140k |
| 25% | 292 | ~$350k |
| 50% | 584 | ~$700k |

District site licences at a discount above ~8 campuses. **Every one of these dollars comes from schools
that already use ClassCade and already like it** — this is expansion revenue, which is the cheapest
revenue there is.

*These are category-norm figures, not ClearK12 numbers. Test the $1,200 against two real campuses before
it goes on a rate card.*

## What free actually costs

The instinct is that free is expensive. For Loop specifically, it mostly isn't — **but the cost is not
where you'd expect.**

**The weekly insight is cheap and bounded.** One generation per child per week, off a compact signal
digest with a cached system prompt. It is a fixed, predictable, batchable cost, and it is the majority
of the product's value. Templated content — the 5-minute moment, grade expectations, the generic
question bank — costs nothing per user at all; it is written once.

**Ask is the cost risk.** It is on-demand, unbounded, and free by policy. A distressed parent at 2am is
exactly who should be using it, and exactly the usage you can't rate-limit for revenue reasons.
Mitigations that don't compromise the promise: serve the eight-or-so most common concerns from a
written library (already how the prototype works) and only reach for generation on genuine free text;
run Ask on a cheaper model tier than the weekly insight; cache aggressively by concern.

**Measure before committing.** Instrument cost-per-active-family-per-month on a pilot cohort. The model
only works if that number is small relative to $1,200/campus ÷ activated families. Rough sanity check:
one connected campus at 50% family activation is ~225 families, so $1,200/campus gives roughly
**$0.44 per family per month** to cover everything. That is a real constraint, and it is the single
number that decides whether this model is viable.

## The flywheel

```
Parent joins free  →  hits an honest gap ("Curiosity: getting to know")
        →  asks their teacher about ClassCade
        →  school adopts Showdown (free, no procurement friction)
        →  school adds Loop Connected (paid)
        →  every family at that school gets the connected experience
        →  those parents tell parents at other schools
```

It runs the other direction too: a teacher already using Showdown means every family at that school is
one invite away from a free Loop account, and a campus with high free-Loop activation is a campus that
has already made the case for the paid upgrade to itself.

**The free side compounds. The paid side converts.** Neither works alone.

---

## What you give up

**The Root & Rise Phase-1 thesis dies.** That plan was explicitly "prove parents will engage weekly and
pay, with zero school dependency." Going free means you can never run that experiment. Replace the
metric: **weekly-active-parent rate** becomes the proof that the standalone product is independently
valuable — and it's arguably the better signal anyway, since willingness to pay for consumer edtech
measures household budget as much as it measures product value.

**Revenue now depends entirely on institutional sales cycles.** No revenue until a school buys, and
school buying is slow and seasonal. Loop cannot fund itself in year one; it has to be funded as an
expansion play on ClassCade.

**Churn becomes district-shaped.** Losing one district loses hundreds of families at once, and those
families lose the half of the product they liked most. Have an answer for what a family sees the month
after their school stops paying — it should be a graceful fall back to free, never a dark screen.

**Free is a one-way door.** You can move a paid product to free. You essentially cannot move a free
product to paid. Decide once.

## So what, if anything, does a parent pay for?

Three candidate answers. One of them is a trap.

### ✗ Parents do not pay for the school connection

Tempting — it's the most obviously valuable thing — and it should be ruled out on principle, not on
pricing.

- **It makes school signal a luxury good.** Two children in the same class, one family sees the
  classroom picture and one doesn't. Teachers will hate it, and a district procurement officer will
  spot the equity problem in the first meeting.
- **You'd be charging a parent for information about their own child that the school already
  generated.** Even where that's legally clean, it is a headline waiting to happen — parents have
  statutory rights to their children's educational records, and monetising access to teacher
  observations sits far too close to that line.
- **It kills the loop.** The free parent who asks their school about ClassCade is the cheapest lead in
  the business. Charge them for the thing they're asking for and they churn instead of advocating.

**Principle to state out loud: a family never pays for school data. The school pays for the seam.**

### ✗ Do not paywall "AI features"

This is the trap, and it fails on three separate grounds.

**It guts the free tier.** The generation *is* the product. The weekly noticing, the grounded question,
the interpretation of a signal into a sentence — take those away and free Loop is six dimension labels
with no read on them. That's not a free tier, it's a teaser, and a teaser doesn't produce advocates.

**It's a depreciating paywall.** "It's AI" was a premium in 2023. By the time Loop is at scale it will
be table stakes, and you'll have priced on the implementation instead of on the outcome. Price on what
the parent gets, never on how it was made.

**The cost argument doesn't even support it.** The weekly insight is the *cheap* part — one bounded,
batchable generation per child per week. The expensive part is Ask, which is on-demand and unbounded —
and Ask is the one thing that must stay free permanently for safety reasons. Paywalling "AI" would
charge for the cheap half and give away the expensive half.

### ✓ What a parent might legitimately pay for

The test for any candidate: **if this were free, would it make more parents advocate to their school?**
If yes, it's free. What survives that test is anything that only matters to a *specific, motivated
minority* and that no school will ever buy on their behalf.

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Time depth** — free keeps a rolling window (the engine already runs 21 days); paid keeps years. *"Confidence, September to June." "The third-grade dip, and when it resolved."* | **Strongest.** Costs storage, compounds with tenure, and takes nothing away from this week's insight. |
| **Portability** — a conference packet, a summary for a tutor, therapist, or the next teacher; a real export. | **Good.** A moment of willingness-to-pay rather than a subscription grind, aimed at families already in a support process. |
| **Ask as a coach, not a safety net** — the single four-part answer stays free forever; an ongoing, multi-turn conversation that remembers the child's history is a different product. | **Careful but viable.** The line must be legible: *worry is free, coaching is paid.* |
| **More children** | **No.** Charging per child punishes large families and reads badly in a parenting product. |
| **The daily ritual** — conversation starters, the 5-minute moment | **No.** That's the retention loop. Paywalling the habit kills the thing that makes free worth having. |
| **Anything safety-adjacent** | **Never.** |

### The recommendation: don't ship a parent paid tier in v1

Free is a one-way door; *adding* a paid tier later is easy, provided you never promise "everything, free,
forever." Ship free + school-paid, instrument what motivated parents actually ask for, and build the
paid tier from evidence rather than from this table. Depth and portability are the leading hypotheses,
not decisions — and if they land, ~$4–6/mo or ~$40/yr is the right neighbourhood, below the original
$7.99, because it is deliberately *less* than the whole product.

## The segment this model abandons

Families whose school will *never* buy ClassCade — private, parochial, homeschool, and small rural
districts with no budget — get the free app forever and generate no revenue, ever. Today that is fine:
they are cheap to serve and they are word-of-mouth. If it becomes a large share of the base, the answer
is a **Family+** tier sold on depth rather than on school data (multi-year history, sibling comparison,
deeper Ask, a coaching layer) — never by taking away something that was free.

Do not build it in v1. Do note it, so the free tier isn't designed in a way that forecloses it.

---

## What has to be true

1. **Cost per active family per month lands under ~$0.40.** Measure on a pilot before pricing anything.
2. **Enough free parents actually ask their school.** Instrument this directly — a tracked
   "Ask your school about ClassCade" action is the single most important metric in the model.
3. **Schools will pay for family engagement they currently get for free-ish.** The pitch is *"we turn
   what your teachers already notice into parent-friendly communication automatically"* — never
   "track student development."
4. **COPPA stays simple.** Free at consumer scale means far more accounts with no school in the consent
   chain. The decision to park the child-facing surface makes this model much safer than it would
   otherwise be; keep it parked.

## What I'd do next

1. Pick two friendly campuses from the existing 1,167. Turn Loop on free for every family.
2. Instrument three numbers: weekly-active-parent rate, "ask your school" clicks, cost per family.
3. Quote the second campus $1,200 and find out whether the number survives contact.
4. Only then flip the concept page.
